What to look at:
  • Load time in each header — same bytes, parsed twice. Mol*'s first number includes ~300 ms of one-time plugin boot; every later change is cheaper.
  • Cartoon quality. 3Dmol draws proper secondary-structure cartoons (arrows on strands, tubes on loops); Mol* draws the more refined of the two. Compare on 1IGT, which is nearly all β-sheet — the arrowheads and the strand twist are what to watch. Then step down the Backbone group: cartoon → flat ribbon → tube is a ladder of how much structure the drawing asserts, and the tube is the honest one when the secondary structure is a guess.
  • Putty is the one backbone style only Mol* has: tube radius driven by B-factor, so the floppy parts of the model look floppy. On 1AON the difference between the rigid equatorial domain and the mobile apical one is the whole picture. 3Dmol's note says so rather than drawing a plain tube and hoping.
  • Surfaces — four of them, and the differences are chemistry. SES (molecular surface) is where a solvent probe can touch; SAS is the probe's own centre, so it is fatter by a water radius; van der Waals ignores the probe entirely, so the pits between atoms stay open. Gaussian is a smoothed approximation, fast enough for big files, and Mol*-only here. Try all four on 2POR — the barrel's pore either survives or fills in. On 2HHB, SES took 5.7 s in 3Dmol and 9.4 s in Mol*, but Mol* builds on a worker and stays responsive while it does. Surfaces are the one capability our own renderer also lacks — see the gaps list in docs/bio-rendering-thorough.md.
  • Colour by hydrophobicity on 2HHB is the closest thing here to the sickle lesson's picture: greasy residues cluster inside, polar ones face the water. Compare it against by-element, which says nothing about the fold at all.
  • Big structures. 1AON is the honest test of whether either can carry a lesson on a school Chromebook — and the one where "gaussian surface" versus "molecular surface" stops being an aesthetic choice.
  • Ligands. 3CYT's heme and 1ATN's ATP — do they survive the cartoon representation, and are they coloured usefully? 2HHB has four haems, one per subunit, so it asks the same question four times over. Both viewers here need the ligand added as a separate component; neither does it for you.
  • Weight. Beside each library's name is what this page actually downloaded for it — unpacked first, since that is the JavaScript the browser has to parse and keep, then the gzipped figure in brackets. Read it next to the <script> tag our own lessons carry: Three.js r128 minified is 603 KB and scene.js plus folding/ribbon.js are 70 KB together, so a viewer here is the largest single thing a lesson would ship.
  • Licence. 3Dmol.js is BSD-3 and Mol* is MIT, so either could have shipped. ChemDoodle Web, which used to be the third pane, is GPLv3 — the page loading it inherits GPL terms — and that is why it is gone from here. No page in this repo loads it any more; see demos/docs/rendering-modules.md.
  • Why keep this page, given we adopted neither. The decision was structural, not aesthetic: both viewers bring their own WebGL context and camera, so neither can draw into scene.js's scene alongside our FX, raycasting and shared zoom ladder. What survives is this page as a style reference — when we build a representation ourselves, these two are the convention to measure it against. Read the Style menu as a spec, not as a shortlist.
