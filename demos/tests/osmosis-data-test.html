Osmosis student data — test bench

One class's potato cores, 8 teams × 6 sucrose concentrations, 48 measurements. Nothing on this page is a typed result: the water potential, every mean, the crossing, and the volume of the cell in panel C are all computed from DATA at load. Change a row and the cell plasmolyses somewhere else.

A — where the answer is

The class knows the answer; no team does. Each dot is one core. The line is the fit through the class means over 0.0–0.4 M, where the response is still linear, and where it crosses zero is the sucrose the potato is already in equilibrium with. Click a team to isolate it and read its own crossing — the spread between teams is nearly twofold, and every one of them ran the same protocol.

B — the curve gives up

Each extra 0.2 M buys less than the last. Segment slopes of the class mean, in % of mass per molar. If osmosis simply tracked the gradient these would all be the same height. They collapse, because past about 0.6 M the protoplast has pulled off the wall and there is little water left to give up. The flattening is the cell, not the chemistry.

C — the same numbers, as a cell

Driven by the data, not by a tuned constant. The protoplast's volume is the class mean % mass change at the slider's molarity, interpolated between the six measured points. The wall is rigid, so above the crossing the extra water becomes pressure and the cell cannot get bigger. The gap opens where the class means cross zero, at — near but not identical to panel A's fitted , because a straight line through four points is not the points. Drag to orbit.

D — the teams, and where they disagree

Two signatures, same three teams. Left: each team's own water potential from its own 0.0–0.4 M fit. Right: how far the class scatters at each concentration. The disagreement is worst at the two ends and nearly vanishes in the middle — and the teams that read most negative are the same ones that fall off the bottom at 1.0 M. That is a technique difference propagating, which is a thing a student can find.
