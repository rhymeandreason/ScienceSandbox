# Reading, per protein

Three sources each, where three exist:

* **PDB-101 Molecule of the Month** — \~600 words, Goodsell illustrations, names the PDB ids it draws, so it lines up with each entry's `source` in `proteins.js`.
* **Nobel Facts** *(nobelprize.org)* — a paragraph and a portrait. Marked **Nobel** below. Popular-information pages exist only for recent prizes (2008 GFP has one; 1962, 1972, 1997 return 404), so Facts is the consistent choice. Three proteins here have no Nobel: collagen, hexokinase, ferritin.
* **One long read** — only where one is better than the summary.

All free. science.org sits behind a bot check, so nothing there is linked or verifiable from a session; a paywalled publisher was replaced rather than linked.

## Myoglobin

* [MotM #1: Myoglobin](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/1) — first entry in the series, for the first protein solved
* **Nobel: Chemistry 1962**, shared with Perutz, for the structure of globular proteins. [Kendrew, Facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1962/kendrew/facts/)
* [de Chadarevian, *John Kendrew and myoglobin*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5980623/) — sperm whale meat, early digital computers, the plasticine sausage

## Hemoglobin

`hemoglobin-lab`.

* [MotM #41: Hemoglobin](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/41) — shape change as the whole mechanism
* **Nobel: Chemistry 1962**, shared with Kendrew. [Perutz, Facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1962/perutz/facts/)

## Collagen

No Nobel.

* [MotM #4: Collagen](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/4)
* [How a Madras scientist won the global race to crack collagen](https://scroll.in/magazine/833341/how-a-madras-scientist-won-the-global-race-in-the-50s-to-crack-the-structure-of-collagen) — Ramachandran vs Rich & Crick, decided by one hydrogen bond
* [The Madras Group and the structure of collagen](https://www.ias.ac.in/article/fulltext/jcsc/111/01/0005-0012) — technical version

## RNase

* [MotM #105: Ribonuclease A](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/105)
* **Nobel: Chemistry 1972**, shared with Moore and Stein, for sequence and conformation in ribonuclease. [Anfinsen, Facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1972/anfinsen/facts/)
* [The Thermodynamic Hypothesis of Protein Folding](https://www.jbc.org/article/S0021-9258\(19\)56522-X/fulltext) — JBC Classic, two pages, sequence determines structure

## Insulin

* [MotM #14: Insulin](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/14) · [#194: Designer Insulins](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/194)
* **Nobel: Chemistry 1958** (Sanger, unshared, for the insulin sequence) and **Chemistry 1964** (Hodgkin, unshared, for X-ray structures of biochemical substances). [Sanger, Facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1958/sanger/facts/) · [Hodgkin, Facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1964/hodgkin/facts/)
* [100 years of insulin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8802620/) — Sanger's ten years, Hodgkin's thirty-four

## GFP

* [MotM #42: GFP](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/42)
* **Nobel: Chemistry 2008**, split three ways for the discovery and development of GFP. [Shimomura](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2008/shimomura/facts/) · [Chalfie](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2008/chalfie/facts/) · [Tsien](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2008/tsien/facts/), Facts. Also the only [popular information](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2008/popular-information/) page on this list
* [Asimov Press, GFP](https://press.asimov.com/articles/gfp)

## Prion

* [MotM #101: Prions](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/101)
* **Nobel: Physiology or Medicine 1997**, unshared, for prions as a new principle of infection. The one prize here that is not Chemistry. [Prusiner, Facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1997/prusiner/facts/)
* [Asimov Press, On Prions and Protein Design](https://www.asimov.press/p/prions)

## ATP synthase

* [MotM #72: ATP Synthase](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/72) — two rotary motors coupled
* **Nobel: Chemistry 1997**, half shared by Boyer and Walker for the enzymatic mechanism of ATP synthesis; Skou took the other half the same year. [Boyer, Facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1997/boyer/facts/) · [Walker, Facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1997/walker/facts/)
* [Direct observation of the rotation of F1-ATPase](https://www.cell.com/fulltext/S0092-8674\(00\)81142-3) — 120° steps, occasional backstep

## Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase

* [MotM #118: Sodium-Potassium Pump](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/118)
* **Nobel: Chemistry 1997**, the half beside Boyer and Walker, for the first ion-transporting enzyme. [Skou, Facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1997/skou/facts/)
* [Jens Skou, Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://www.mayoclinicproceedings.org/article/S0025-6196\(13\)00283-8/fulltext) — crab nerve membranes, and insisting a protein spans the bilayer before that was allowed

## Hexokinase

No Nobel.

* [MotM #50: Glycolytic Enzymes](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/50) — shared page; hexokinase is the clamp
* [Koshland, key-lock vs induced fit](https://www2.tulane.edu/~biochem/lecture/gbch701/koshland2.pdf) (PDF)
* [Glucose-induced conformational change in yeast hexokinase](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.75.10.4848) — PNAS 1978, the structure that confirmed the clamp

## Chymotrypsin

* [MotM #46: Trypsin](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/46) — no chymotrypsin page; same fold, same Ser-His-Asp triad
* **Nobel: Chemistry 1946**, shared with Sumner and Stanley, for preparing enzymes in pure crystalline form. [Northrop, Facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1946/northrop/facts/) — crystallizing enzymes, chymotrypsin among them
* [Chymotrypsin, the first protease](https://www.iycr2014.org/learn/crystallography365/articles/20140812) — Blow's structure, fourth ever solved, mechanism visible on sight

## Ferritin

No Nobel, no narrative. The weakest field of the eleven.

* [MotM #35: Ferritin and Transferrin](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/35)
* [Structural biology of ferritin nanocages](https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/1873-3468.70302) — FEBS Letters 2026, CC-BY
