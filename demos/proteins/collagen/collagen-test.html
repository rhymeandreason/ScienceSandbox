The helix nobody records. A PDB says helix or sheet, and the collagen helix is neither — it is polyproline II, three of them coiled together. Half of these six files therefore carry no secondary structure at all and bake as pure coil, which is honest and is why the strands are told apart by colour instead. Two of the three that do carry records are describing their PARTNER — the integrin domain and Hsp47 are ordinary proteins with real helices and sheets in them, and the collagen beside them is still coil. That leaves 1CAG, the only file here that annotates a collagen chain at all, as a helix of class 10 and a coiled coil in the remark. So the collection holds both answers about the same shape, and the ss records row says which one you are looking at.

Hydroxyproline is deposited as HETATM, because it is a modified residue. It is also every third residue of collagen: an ATOM-only trace of 1CAG keeps 19 of 29 and splines the ribbon over the holes, looking like a disordered protein rather than a broken read. The bakes pass each file's own MODRES set, and the hydroxylated row is what that bought.

A third of the protein in you, and the reason skin, bone, tendon and cornea hold their shape. Three chains, each a left-handed polyproline helix, wound into a right-handed rope.

The sequence is Gly-X-Y, forever: every third residue is glycine because the three chains pack so close to the axis that nothing with a side chain fits there. X is usually proline and Y usually hydroxyproline, which is why you need vitamin C — the enzyme that makes hydroxyproline needs it, and without it the helix is unstable at body temperature. That is scurvy: not a missing protein, a protein that cannot hold its shape.

The glycine rule is what makes the disease. Swap one glycine for alanine — one extra methyl group, the smallest possible change — and the helix cannot close there. In a person that is osteogenesis imperfecta, and it is dominant rather than recessive: the bad chain joins triple helices built mostly from good chains and ruins them, so one broken copy out of two is worse than having none.

Then it leaves the cell and assembles: molecules staggered by 67 nm into fibrils, fibrils into fibres, cross-linked until they stop being individual molecules at all. That is the step no deposition holds, and the step that makes tendon.
