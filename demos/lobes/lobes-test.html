lobes.js — test bench

Four mounts of the same module. Nothing on this page types an angle: every ear's direction is solved from the molecule's own bond vectors, and every count from a formal-electron sum over bond orders.

A — water, and why ice is tetrahedral. Drag to orbit. The two ears do not lie in the H−O−H plane, they straddle it — two bonds down, two pairs up and back, four domains at the tetrahedral angle. Fill the slots and watch the oxygen run out at two: that cap is already in water-lab's engine, and this is the picture of it.

B — a carbonyl oxygen is sideways. Carbonic acid. The C=O oxygen is sp², not sp³: a double bond is one domain, so it has three, and its two pairs sit in the plane at 120°. A donor comes in from the side. The two −OH oxygens next to it are muted for the opposite reason — they sit against a π system, and one of their pairs is in it.

C — AMP, where the module refuses. The phosphate oxygens get nothing. Their electron count comes out at 2.5 because the −2 is delocalised across them and every P–O here is a single stick (SCIENCE.md §3). Drawing ears would assert a localisation the rest of the repo deliberately does not draw, so the answer is null and a reason.

D — adenine. The one to spend time on. Five nitrogens, and they are not interchangeable. Three carry a blue ear in the ring plane — those accept. Two are grey, and lying flat against the ring: their pairs are in the π system, so the amino group has nothing to accept with and donates instead. That asymmetry is base pairing.
