Test — the HbS fibre, at three scales.
Green = measured from PDB 2HBS, the deoxy-HbS crystal itself. Amber = modelled from EM reconstructions, which this repo has no copy of and cannot check.

The contact and the double strand are measured. 2HBS is P2₁ with two tetramers per asymmetric unit, and those two are the lateral pair — β6 of chain H sits in the Phe85/Leu88 pocket of chain B. The pair then repeats along the crystallographic a axis, and under −a, β6 of chain D reaches the pocket of chain F. So one rigid motion and one translation, both read off the file, generate a double strand of any length.

The 14-strand fibre is not. Seven double strands, their radii, and the helical twist come from electron-microscope work, not from this crystal. They are sliders here rather than constants, because that is what an unverified number should look like.

Watch strain in the readout as you move the pitch. Twisting rigid copies of a crystal strand stretches every β6-to-pocket contact by the same amount — a real fibre absorbs that by deforming, these copies can't, so past about 2 Å the picture has stopped modelling anything.
