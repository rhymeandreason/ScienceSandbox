cell.js — test bench

Four mounts of the same module. Nothing on this page types a surface area, a volume or a depth: each one is summed over the triangles of the surface in front of you.

A — size, with the camera held still. The frame does not refit and the wheel does not zoom. A size slider on a camera that follows it is a zoom control and reads as one, which is the failure this page would otherwise ship with. The dashed outline is a red blood cell at true scale, drawn by the same module, laid over the subject like a caliper, and it does not move.

B — shape, with the volume held still. Same cytoplasm throughout: the module measures the volume and rescales onto the target, so every shape here holds exactly …. Only the surface and the depth move. Sit with red cell — a dimple puts every part of it within a micrometre of the outside without giving up any volume.

C — folding. Volume held still again. Watch the two readouts move apart: surface climbs steeply, and the deepest point stays where it was. Folding answers the surface budget and answers nothing about the shape of the cytoplasm underneath it. The cap slider decides which cell you are looking at. Wide open, the fingers cover everything and this is a lymphocyte in circulation, or anything rounded up in suspension. Closed down, they sit on one face and it is an enterocyte or a kidney proximal tubule cell, whose brush border faces the lumen and nowhere else.

D — six real cells, measured at load. Each row is built from its own parameters and measured, not looked up, and its note types no number the columns already carry. fold is the surface over the least a bag of that volume could have got away with.
