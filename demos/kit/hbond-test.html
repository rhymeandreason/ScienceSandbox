hbond.js — test bench

Two mounts of the same module, with no lesson around it. The matching logic is asserted offline by node kit/check-kit.js; what is left is whether the bond reads as directional, and that is what this page is for.

A — direction, at a fixed distance. Neither slider moves the molecules any closer or further apart, and both can still change the answer — which is the whole difference between a hydrogen bond and "two molecules near each other".

Tilt turns the donor in place, and it is a hard gate: a bond needs its O–H pointing roughly at the acceptor. The bond dies around 45° and comes back near 80° — on the donor's other hydrogen, which the same turn has swung into range.

Approach walks the donor around the acceptor and is deliberately not a hard gate. Off-axis costs alignment, not existence: real hydrogen bonds sit broadly around an acceptor rather than in tight cones about idealised lone pairs, so the readout reports how well aimed the bond is instead of pretending 60° is a wall. Keep going past 90° and it does die — that is the approach arriving behind the ears, where the oxygen's own hydrogens are.

B — adenine donates where it cannot accept. Every nitrogen here is drawn the same way in every textbook figure. They are not the same. The exocyclic amino nitrogen has a lone pair by the electron count and cannot use it — it is in the ring's π system — so it appears below as a donor and, with capacity 0, never as an acceptor. Get this wrong and A pairs with the wrong thing.
