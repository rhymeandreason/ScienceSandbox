glucose chains — test bench

One linkage, measured off the real disaccharide, applied over and over. Nothing here draws a helix or a ribbon: a rigid repeat is always a rotation about an axis plus a slide along it, so whatever shape appears is what that glycosidic bond does when you do it twelve times.

Cellulose — β-1,4. The bridge leaves C1 equatorial, in the ring's plane, so each residue hands off to the next straight ahead. Half a turn per residue is a flat two-fold ribbon, which is what stacks into fibres nothing human can digest.

Starch — α-1,4. The bridge leaves C1 axial, out of the plane, so each residue should hand off at an angle and the chain should curl into a helix loose enough for amylase to reach into. It does not, here. Read the note above.
